## Register entries

### Method

Every entry was checked on 24 October 2026 by AI research agents working for the maintainer, from public pages only: no form was submitted and no account created. No person has re-checked them. Under the register's quality definition (plan section 5), an agent check with the evidence recorded below counts as `verified`.

- **Contact:** each channel was taken from the entity's own privacy policy, credit reporting policy or contact page, listed in the entry's `sources`. Every channel (email, post, phone, web form and manual-check page) was then matched against the saved text of those pages or, for links, a query of the page's links.
- **ABN:** each ABN was read from the entity's own pages or found on ABN Lookup by name, then re-checked on ABN Lookup; all 29 were active.
- **Leads:** no M1 entry came from the Optery directory or the Big Ass Data Broker Opt-Out List. Those lists were used only to learn the names of people-search sites, all of which were rejected below; 2 of them returned at M2 as overseas entries. No text, URL or description was copied from any lead source.

'M1 seed list' means the firm was named in the milestone's research brief: the ACCC report firms, the credit reporting bodies and well-known Australian property, directory and loyalty businesses.

### Evidence hashes

At M1, 42 of the 71 source pages had an `[[evidence]]` hash; the 3 overseas entries added at M2 bring 15 more (57 across 25 entries), and the additions of 26 October 2026 bring 52 more (109 across 58 entries; see that section for their untracked pages). The M1 hashes were taken on 24 October 2026 with `cargo run -p xtask -- evidence-hash` and confirmed by a second fetch. Five hashes were recomputed the same day after normaliser fixes: 2 Australia Post pages and 2 Woolworths Group privacy pages, where markup inside quoted attribute values had leaked into the text, and Equifax's credit and identity products page, whose nested `<main>` had been cut short. On the same fetched pages the earlier normaliser reproduced each original hash, so the pages themselves had not changed. The other pages are not tracked by the monthly drift check, because a plain HTTP fetch gets an error, a bot check or a page that needs a browser:

- whole entries: `domain-au` (CoStar returns 403), `experian-au` and `experian-marketing-au` (every page returns the same bot-check page), `flybuys-au` (script-only page and a malformed response), `liveramp-au` (its domains did not resolve from the checking machine; the TrustArc form needs a browser), `priceline-pulse-rewards-au` (script-only pages) and `rea-group-au` (429 and 403);
- single pages: the Equifax complaints form, the Lead Lists and List Factory contact forms, the TaleFin portal, InfoTrack's policy URL that redirects to the tracked PDF, Thryv's privacy policy and White Pages (403), and the WINR request form.

### Notes on individual entries

- `acceleon-au`, `conexum-au`, `list-factory-au`: the ABN the entity publishes, or the one ABN Lookup gives for its business name, belongs to a trust, so ABN Lookup shows the trustee. The entity's own pages do not say whether the company named in its privacy policy is that trustee; an ASIC check would settle it. Letters use the company name in the privacy policy.
- `experian-au`: Experian Data Registries Pty Ltd, formerly illion Data Registries, is the credit reporting body; illion no longer runs a separate one.
- `equifax-marketing-au`, `experian-marketing-au`: separate legal entities from the credit reporting bodies, with their own policies.
- `domain-au`: domain.com.au's privacy link redirects to the privacy notice of its owner, CoStar, which has an Australian section; its contact table gives privacy@domain.com.au for Domain services, naming domain.com.au and Allhomes. Pricefinder's site links to the same policy, but its operating entity was not confirmed, so the entry does not claim to cover it.
- `rea-group-au`: PropTrack shares REA Group's privacy policy, but PropTrack Pty Ltd is a separate company, so the entry does not claim to cover it.
- `thryv-au`: White Pages says residential listing removal and silent numbers go through the person's phone provider, so the entry is in the marketing pack (for Thryv Data) rather than the people-search pack; the hygiene checklist will cover the phone provider route.
- `talefin-au`: Part IIIA requests go through its consumer portal, which needs an account. The entry also lists its general email address, which its pages do not name as a channel for Part IIIA requests.
- `quantium-au`, `nielsen-au`: Quantium's policy says it does not sell personal information and describes only business-to-business direct marketing; Nielsen's panel policy says it will not use identifying data to market to panel members and does not let clients do so. The entries therefore carry APP 13 levers only and are in no pack.
- `landchecker-au`: the privacy contact is its support inbox; small business exemption possible.
- `globalx-au`: the policy is dated 1 July 2021 and is issued by Dye & Durham Australia Pty Ltd for its affiliates; its postal contact is the Data Protection Officer at Dye & Durham Australia Property Limited.
